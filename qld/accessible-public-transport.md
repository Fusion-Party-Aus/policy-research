# Make Public Transport Accessible

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-01  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PUBLIC SERVICES

Public transport accessibility obligations would link a published register of verified barriers to a funded remediation program, with responsible asset owners, delivery dates and independent compliance checks covering complete passenger journeys.

### The Problem

An accessible vehicle is not enough if a passenger cannot reach the stop, board safely, change services or obtain usable information. Queensland should organise upgrades around complete journeys and the barriers identified by passengers.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Cost surveys, design, construction, replacement transport and ongoing maintenance separately. Coordinate with asset renewal, but disclose additional spending and displaced projects. No statewide dollar estimate is asserted.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Transport capital and accessibility programs, with maintenance and renewal budgets. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Visible barriers** — Publish an asset-level register of verified barriers, ownership, assessment dates, funding status and planned remedies.
- **Complete journeys** — Prioritise continuous accessible journeys to health care, education, employment and essential services, with disability organisations helping set priorities.
- **Accessible facilities** — Include paths, crossings, lighting, boarding areas, lifts, toilets where relevant, hearing support and readable passenger information in project scopes.
- **Replacement travel** — Require accessible replacement services and clear advance advice during planned closures; publish arrangements for unplanned disruption.
- **Funded repairs** — Fund repeatable small works in a rolling program and independently check completed upgrades with users before closing each register entry.

**Design Rationale**

A journey can fail at a path, boarding point, interchange or passenger-information interface even when the vehicle is accessible. Asset-level reporting needs to distinguish an isolated improvement from a continuous usable journey. Compliance assessment must therefore use the applicable legal standards rather than treating a completed construction project as proof of accessibility.

**System Interaction**

Responsibility follows asset ownership; a council path and an operator vehicle may form parts of the same journey without having the same owner. Compliance records need assessment dates and a clear distinction between verified barriers and unassessed assets. Disability organisations can test practical usability without replacing formal legal assessment. Temporary closures require accessible alternatives within the same journey obligation.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Accessibility investment has both capital and maintenance costs. Coordinating it with asset renewal can avoid repeated works, but does not remove the cost of making an entire journey usable.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Co-design with people with varied disabilities. Maintain assistance during works. Record exemptions and unresolved barriers openly without publishing personal passenger information.

**Evidence & Precedent**

Translink publishes accessibility information and a Disability Action Plan. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Obtain the verified backlog, asset ownership, legal compliance assessment, works rates and maintenance history before setting completion targets.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](https://github.com/Fusion-Party-Aus/FusionPolicy/blob/qld-policy-drafts-2026-10-07/QLD/Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: reconcile asset ownership, validate barriers and agree assessment methods with users. Months 6-18: publish and fund the first priority corridors; deliver straightforward repairs alongside detailed design. Years 2-4: expand the program, report quarterly and independently audit completed journeys. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
