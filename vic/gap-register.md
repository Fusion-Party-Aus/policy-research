# Victorian policy gap register

> **Status:** Initial assessment — not peer-reviewed
> **Jurisdiction:** Victoria, Australia
> **Generated:** 2026-10-16
> **AI assistance:** Gaps identified by Policy Researcher agent based on corpus analysis. This is a bounded initial assessment identifying areas where evidence is thin, missing, or where policy areas common to state platforms are not addressed. It does not represent exhaustive research. Political value choices (e.g. which policy areas to prioritise) are distinguished from evidence gaps.

## Priority key

| Priority | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| **High** | Policy makes specific claims with insufficient or no cited evidence, or a major policy area is entirely absent |
| **Medium** | Evidence exists but is incomplete, dated, or from other jurisdictions without transferability analysis |
| **Low** | Minor gaps, refinements, or areas where existing evidence is adequate but could be strengthened |

## Policy areas not addressed

The following areas are commonly addressed in Victorian state policy platforms but do not appear 
in the current corpus. The absence of a policy may reflect a deliberate political choice rather 
than an oversight — this register does not recommend which areas to adopt, only identifies where 
research might be useful if the party decides to develop positions.

| # | Area | Priority | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Health and hospitals | High | No health, mental health or hospital policy despite being a major state responsibility. Research needed: Victorian hospital waiting times, mental health system capacity, preventive health economics. |
| 2 | Education and skills | High | No education, TAFE, school funding or early childhood policy. Research needed: Victorian school funding models, TAFE system review, early childhood access and quality. |
| 3 | Environment, climate and energy | High | No environment, climate, emissions reduction or energy policy despite Victorian legislation (Climate Change Act 2017). Research needed: Victorian emissions trajectory, renewable energy economics, biodiversity and land clearing. |
| 4 | Water and natural resources | Medium | No water, catchment or resource management policy. Research needed: Victorian water pricing, desalination costs, catchment health. |
| 5 | Aboriginal affairs and treaty | Medium | No policy addressing the Victorian Treaty process, self-determination or Closing the Gap. Research needed: Treaty Act 2018 implementation, First Peoples' Assembly outcomes. |
| 6 | Emergency management | Medium | No bushfire, flood or emergency management policy. Research needed: Victorian emergency management capability, climate adaptation infrastructure. |
| 7 | Justice and corrections | Medium | Youth diversion and policing are covered, but adult corrections, prison overcrowding and reoffending are not addressed. Research needed: Victorian prison population trends, recidivism data, justice reinvestment evidence. |
| 8 | Regional development | Low | Transport connections to regions are covered, but broader regional economic development, service delivery and population policy are not. Research needed: regional service access, population distribution trends. |
| 9 | Arts, culture and creative industries | Low | Not addressed. Research needed: Victorian creative industry economics, cultural infrastructure access. |
| 10 | Disability and social services | Low | Accessible transport is covered, but broader disability services, NDIS interface with state services and social housing are not. Research needed: NDIS state interaction, social housing waiting lists. |

## Cross-cutting research needs

| # | Topic | Priority | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Independent costing and fiscal impact | High | Most policies describe costs qualitatively. A Parliamentary Budget Office costing or independent fiscal model would substantially strengthen credibility. No policy has a quantified net fiscal impact. |
| 2 | Distributional impact analysis | Medium | Several policies (land tax, value capture, franchise reform) have distributional consequences but no explicit distributional modelling is referenced. |
| 3 | Implementation sequencing and dependencies | Medium | Many policies cross-reference each other (e.g. transport, housing, infrastructure) but no single document maps implementation dependencies or sequencing trade-offs. |
| 4 | Victorian demographic and spatial data baseline | Low | A shared spatial and demographic evidence base (population projections, growth areas, service access) would support multiple policies and avoid duplication. |
| 5 | International comparison methodology | Low | Several policies draw on Japanese, Singaporean or other international examples. A consistent transferability framework would strengthen these comparisons. |

## Summary

- **18** of 41 policies have no linked research resource
- **31** policies have specific evidence quality observations
- **10** common state policy areas are not addressed
- **5** cross-cutting research needs identified

The strongest evidence cluster is in public safety and justice (6 policies, 6 dedicated research 
resources) and transport/infrastructure (12 policies, 9 research resources). The largest gap is 
the complete absence of health, education and environment/energy policy, which are typically the 
three largest areas of Victorian state expenditure and responsibility.
