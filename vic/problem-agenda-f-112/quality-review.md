## Strongest challenges and disposition

1. **Selection may reflect the analyst's frame.** Eleven scored problems are not all Victorian problems; groups with sparse data can be excluded by evidence availability. VP-012 and other domain gaps are retained, with an urgent baseline tranche. The housing-first suggestion remains conditional on human acceptance.
2. **Numbers can conceal values.** Ordinal criteria are not comparable welfare measurements; high uncertainty might favour undermeasurement; ecological loss is poorly represented by human inequity. Weight-only and rating-change scenarios are supplied. Differences of a few points are not reliable; no statistical significance or optimal resource allocation is claimed.
3. **Scale judgements are particularly weak.** The housing and mental-health scale ratings can change the queue. One-point housing-scale reduction shifts first place to family violence; ecological inequity/value rating 5 shifts it to biodiversity. The frozen ratings are retained as an auditable hypothesis, not silently corrected to defend housing.
4. **A narrow first batch can leave the most severe housing need unstudied.** Tenancy prevention is not the full homelessness agenda. Existing rough sleeping, social-housing supply, unsafe households and institutional exits remain separate strata/gaps. A tenancy-retention result must measure safety and affordability, not just a lease continuing.
5. **Evidence of need is not evidence of a response.** The contextual thermal-upgrade trial cannot establish eviction prevention. Null hospital/ED findings prevent an inference of demonstrated acute-care savings. No retention-effect verdict or comparative benefit was generated.
6. **Apparent improvement may be real or artefactual.** Incident reporting, service reallocation, cohort changes and population growth can alter indicators. Recorded family incidents declined in the used window; suicide counts fluctuate; road rates compare favourably nationally; childhood outcomes remain above national. These countercases are retained, with no causal reform claim.
7. **The corpus cannot adjudicate the agenda.** Direct overlap is strongest in transport; omitted dedicated responses may reflect export scope or coding limits. All original IDs are retained, and 27/66 records with no specific association are not called irrelevant. Ranking v1 stays unchanged after mapping.

## Citation verification boundary

All 18 retained source records have an agency/publisher, title, reference period, URL, locator, access state and limitation. Source–problem rows reference valid source IDs; registered source links, mappings and snapshots are checked by `validate_agenda.py`. There are 20 evidence rows, not 20 independent sources. None of the corpus's 66 records was independently evidence-assessed.

S16 identity was confirmed in Europe PMC XML and PubMed: Katie Page and co-authors; BMJ Open 15(2), e082340; published 26 February 2025; DOI `https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2023-082340`. A draft metadata date was corrected from 27 to 26 February before the freeze. One initial access failure did not become a claim of unavailable full text once XML access succeeded. S09's more recent 2024 numbers, rather than an older search snippet, were used. No claim of universal retraction clearance or full bibliography-database validation is made.

Citation conventions are adapted to a repository policy brief, with named source links and CSV metadata. No direct quotations of third-party research findings are required. Primary source content was paraphrased; no restricted full text is reproduced. The public policy-export originals are retained solely for provenance/internal review. Future public reuse still requires the applicable rights and publication checks.

## Remaining acceptance questions

Human review is required for framing/weights, the urgent unranked child-safety gap, first-batch choice, eligibility/configuration and pilot adequacy. Source access exceptions and incomplete subgroup validation are disclosed rather than converted to exclusions or completed coverage. There is enough traceability to review this bounded agenda; there is not enough evidence to adopt or rank interventions.
