## Baseline and alternatives

Problem definition and who set it; units, denominators, geography and data year. State the status quo and counterfactual. Compare at least one incremental and one materially different option; no intervention remains eligible. Identify which ranking depends on human values, rights or acceptable risk rather than an empirical estimate.

## Search and selection record

Engine/catalogue; exact strings and filters; dates/rank limits; imported candidate IDs; duplicate-to-master mappings; eligibility version; include/unclear/exclude decisions and ordered reason; full-text access state; reviewer method and unresolved decisions. State what was not searched and why. Avoid claimed saturation unless established within declared bounds.

Separate publication identity, reachability, full-text access, methodological quality and support for this claim. A source can be authoritative for a statutory definition yet unsuitable for a causal effect. Record null results, harms, conflicting designs and reused underlying data. Never count multiple papers on one dataset as independent replications.

## Mechanism, economics and implementation

Show the proposed causal chain, necessary assumptions, rival explanations and what observation would falsify it. For economics distinguish capital/operating cost, gross/net benefit, financing/revenue, accounting/cash and public/private incidence. Record price year, discount rate, baseline, horizon, sensitivity, uncertainty and opportunity cost. List missing model inputs without fabricating estimates. Check legal instruments/current commencement, institutions, workforce, procurement, dependencies, staged delivery, accessibility, privacy and civil-liberty effects.

## Counterargument and decision

Strongest credible countercase and better alternatives; what survives the challenge? Are there internal contradictions across related proposals? Which affected groups or outcomes were overlooked? Record the exact evidence supporting the objection with the same burden as a supportive claim.

Verdict for this claim: **well supported / right objective but weak mechanism / unsubstantiated claim / overlooked consequences / better alternative / insufficient evidence**. Mixed policy verdicts require separate atomic assessments. State confidence and reason, uncertainty that could change the decision, and what additional evidence would be decisive. “Unsubstantiated” means support was not established within the search boundary, not proof of falsity. “Better alternative” requires a defined objective, comparator and supported trade-off, not a preference asserted as evidence.

Recommendation to humans: retain, qualify, investigate, test a pilot, compare an alternative or consider amendment; state any political value choice explicitly. Do not edit adopted wording or infer adoption/publication approval. List unresolved questions ranked by significance, evidence uncertainty and feasible next step. Update a versioned coverage ledger with assessed/unassessed denominators; reviewer/approver and acceptance remain human fields.
