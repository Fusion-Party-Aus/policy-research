# Proposed F113-PB01 — preventing loss of safe, affordable tenancies

Status: proposed for human acceptance; no response screening or synthesis has been executed. Problem VP-001; protocol version 1.0, 17 October 2026. Nested outcome: sustained safe, affordable housing for low-income Victorian renters at risk of tenancy loss. This is a research specification, not an adopted policy or recommendation to fund a mechanism.

## Questions and outcomes

- Q01 (baseline): How often do at-risk Victorian renters lose secure tenancies or enter/re-enter homelessness, and how does this differ by household type, Aboriginal status, disability, family violence and metropolitan/regional setting? Establish denominators, dates, repeat episodes and service eligibility.
- Q02 (comparative effect): Relative to current provision or another feasible option, which responses improve safe housing retention at 6, 12 and, where available, 24 months? Consider arrears assistance, legal/advocacy support, integrated case support, access to alternative social/private tenancies and no new intervention. An option need not resemble any Fusion proposal.
- Q03 (harms/equity): Do apparent gains conceal unsafe tenancy retention, landlord capture, increased debt, displacement, exclusion, coercion or burdens on survivors? Whose access worsens? Which null or harmful findings challenge the preferred mechanism?
- Q04 (feasibility/economics): What state powers, workforce, housing availability, recurring/capital costs and opportunity costs constrain useful delivery? Separate household benefit from fiscal saving; record price year, horizon and uncertainty. No invented Victorian cost-effectiveness model.

Primary outcome: proportion of the defined eligible cohort in safe, affordable housing at 12 months, with loss-to-follow-up and deaths accounted for. Report the source's actual outcome definition rather than silently equating tenancy retained with safety or affordability. Secondary: repeat homelessness, eviction, wellbeing, survivor-defined safety, service access, household debt, public/private costs and unequal effects. Obtain lived-experience/Aboriginal governance input through an authorised human process later; no new primary human-subject research is included here.

## Scope and boundaries

Adults/households experiencing low income or financial hardship and at risk of losing a tenancy. Keep people already sleeping rough or exiting institutions in a separate transferability stratum; do not pool automatically. Victoria first; Australian and comparable high-income jurisdictions are eligible with explicit tenancy-law, welfare, market and service transfer assessments. Retain relevant qualitative implementation evidence and systematic reviews as context; do not count a review and its primary studies as independent replications.

Exclude a new statewide housing-supply business case, tax reform modelling and construction recommendations from this first batch. Record them as alternatives outside the current appraisal boundary. The baseline refresh must establish whether newer data change urgency before making intervention recommendations.

## Search plan (proposed, not executed)

Search ABS, AIHW, Homes Victoria/DFFH, VAGO and Productivity Commission for baselines. Search AHURI, AIFS, ANROWS for Australian evaluations and safety interactions; PubMed/Europe PMC, Crossref, Campbell/Cochrane and Google Scholar/web for studies, then backward/forward citations of eligible studies. Paid databases and inaccessible records stay logged, not silently substituted.

Run the following strings, adapting syntax transparently and logging each version and date:

1. `(Victoria OR Australia) (tenancy sustainment OR eviction prevention OR housing retention) (evaluation OR trial OR cohort)`
2. `(rent arrears OR emergency rental assistance OR tenancy legal assistance) (trial OR quasi-experimental OR evaluation) (housing stability OR homelessness)`
3. `(eviction prevention OR tenancy sustainment) (null OR no effect OR adverse OR displacement OR landlord capture OR systematic review)`
4. `(Aboriginal OR disability OR family violence OR rural) (tenancy sustainment OR eviction prevention) (outcome OR evaluation)`
5. `(homelessness prevention OR housing retention) (usual care OR cash assistance OR legal support OR case management) (cost OR cost-effectiveness)`

No Fusion-domain results contribute independent effectiveness evidence. Read status-quo and credible alternative evidence with the same burden as evidence for a favoured response. Reserve at least half the appraisal effort for non-Fusion baselines, alternatives, harms and conflicting results, consistent with shared F-107 controls.

## Finite batch and stopping rules

Maximum **12 research hours**, **80 unique candidate records** and **30 deeply appraised documents**, whichever cap is reached first; at most eight atomic response-effect/feasibility assessments across Q01–Q04. The 12 hours include baseline refresh, screening, appraisal, checking and reporting. Search at most the first 20 returned records per query/engine and no more than one backward/forward citation generation; log rank and engine. Stop retrieval at 80 unique records, but retain all already retrieved records in the ledger and mark unappraised ones pending. These caps describe a limited evidence brief, not search saturation or a systematic review.

The proposed human-labelled calibration set is up to 20 records (all when fewer), deliberately mixing easy, ambiguous and near-miss items. This is smaller than sr-screener's usual large-review pilot because of the bounded agenda task; no full-screening run proceeds automatically. Humans must approve the protocol/config, boundary cases, pilot adequacy and any cost estimate. If the pilot finds unsafe exclusions or the search lacks plausible alternatives, revise scope or return an explicit insufficiency result. Do not increase the budget or create recurring searches.

Stop substantive appraisal and report an access/evidence gap if core eligibility or legal/market transfer cannot be determined. Do not infer a mechanism fails from inaccessible evidence. No scheduled monitoring is authorised.

Allowed verdicts: well supported, right objective but weak mechanism, unsubstantiated within the search boundary, overlooked consequences, better alternative, or insufficient evidence.
