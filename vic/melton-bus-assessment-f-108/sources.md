# Sources and claim-specific appraisals

> **Research — not adopted Fusion policy**

Policy Researcher · 17 October 2026 AEDT · AI-assisted · under human review. All URLs were inspected directly or through the search provider on 16–17 October 2026. “Read” below names the actual scope, not a claim of complete full-text assessment. The 19 source families are distinct documents/datasets; companion landing pages and appendices are not extra studies. The same VAGO/IV data reused across claims are not independent replications. Official law and datasets are assessed for authority/measurement, outside an experimental causal hierarchy. No pooled effect, formal GRADE score or calibrated risk-of-bias instrument is claimed.

## S01

Victorian Auditor-General's Office. (2026, June 17). [*Improving bus services*](https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/report/improving-bus-services), especially sections 1, 3–5 and [Appendix D, pp. D1–D4](https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-26/Improving%20Bus%20Services/20260617_Improving-Bus-Services-Appendix-D.pdf).

**Read:** report HTML and complete methodology appendix. **Design/fitness:** external performance audit, descriptive/analytical (VI); strong for audit definitions, moderate for baseline, no intervention identification. June 2025 transfer analysis uses scheduled services, high-priority samples (12 over two years; over 200 connections over four weeks). Bus-to-bus, school-bell and V/Line connections are excluded. **Author-acknowledged limits:** Appendix D excludes crossing/terrain features and FlexiRide from spatial analysis. **Integrity flag:** section 1 says 68%, section 3 says 73%; raw service-level numerator/denominator not supplied here. **Interest:** parliamentary auditor, not programme operator; underlying DTP data dependence remains. **Victoria:** directly relevant; transfer analysis cannot establish Melton V/Line or all-route performance. Identity verified on official page; metadata alone is not statistical replication.

## S02

Australian Bureau of Statistics. (2021 Census). [*Melton, Census all persons QuickStats*, LGA24650](https://www.abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/quickstats/2021/LGA24650).

**Read:** geography header, population, household vehicles and commuting notes. **Design/fitness:** aggregate Census baseline (VI), high for the specified 2021 geography and denominator, low for 2026 need or intervention effects. Vehicle ownership is household-level, not individual ability to drive. Pandemic conditions limit commuting interpretation; Census values are subject to published statistical processes. **Interest:** official statistics; no transport-service promotion. No new growth estimate or individual inference made. Official identity verified; different Melton geographies retained as distinct candidates.

## S03

Infrastructure Victoria. (2023). [*Fast, frequent, fair: How buses can better connect Melbourne*](https://www.infrastructurevictoria.com.au/resources/fast-frequent-fair-how-buses-can-better-connect-melbourne). [Public PDF](https://assets.infrastructurevictoria.com.au/assets/Resources/Fast-Frequent-Fair-how-buses-can-better-connect-Melbourne-2025.pdf).

**Read:** recommendations 1–4, 7–8, relevant modelling/distributional sections and reference list; PDF available, not every reference checked. Landing page dates the report 2023 despite the current PDF filename containing 2025. **Design/fitness:** policy synthesis, engagement and scenario modelling (VII/analytical), moderate for option generation, insufficient for Melton causal forecasts. Frequency, route directness, span, community transport, stop quality and fares are complementary recommendations. **Interest:** independent statutory adviser commissioning reform research; supportive policy interpretation, not a controlled evaluation. Model estimates share S04's assumptions. Official publisher verified; no modelled benefit is presented as observed.

## S04

WSP. (2023, September). [*Better buses for Melbourne strategic modelling: Validation report*](https://assets.infrastructurevictoria.com.au/assets/Resources/06_WSP-for-Infrastructure-Victoria_Better-buses-for-Melbourne-Strategic-modelling-Validation-report-Sept-2023.pdf).

**Read:** executive summary, §§2–3, 5.4 and 6. **Design/fitness:** calibrated four-step strategic model, analytical rather than causal; moderate for metropolitan scenario sensitivity, low for particular stops or timetables. **Author-acknowledged limits:** §3 lacks detailed pedestrian pathways/stops and trip suppression; executive summary/§6 flag weak transfer validation and speed sensitivity. A model's frequency elasticity is not an observed treatment effect. **Interest:** commissioned consultant evaluating its model improvements for IV; shared evidence family with S03. Public copy verified and privately inspected; boilerplate restrictions/public-release markings retained at source, full PDF not redistributed.

## S07

Transport Victoria. (Undated, retrieved 2026, October 17). [*FlexiRide on-demand bus service*](https://transport.vic.gov.au/plan-a-journey/travel-tips-and-resources/flexiride-on-demand-bus-service).

**Read:** indexed HTML overview and booking/payment sections; local direct request returned 403. **Design/fitness:** current service information, moderate for advertised booking channels, no evidence for historical trip performance or refusal distribution. The Melton South listing permits app/telephone booking up to seven days ahead; that is a maximum advance-booking horizon, not a minimum waiting guarantee. Collapsed area-specific details were not comprehensively extracted. **Interest:** service promoter/administrator. Cannot corroborate C10's three-hour trip, 800-metre final leg, or claims about all services.

## S08

Victoria. (2026 compilation). [*Transport Integration Act 2010*, version 091, effective 1 January 2026](https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/transport-integration-act-2010/091), [authorised text](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/10-6aa091-authorised.pdf).

**Read:** version history; Part 2 §§24–28; Part 3 §§29–34 (authorised pp. 50–61). **Fitness:** authoritative for these provisions, not exhaustive legal advice. Sections 29, 33A and 34 establish general ministerial, procurement, planning and contract powers. Section 28 limits the creation of civil rights by Part 2. **Unresolved:** contract variation terms, appropriations, delegations and the precise power to impose a binding new universal service standard were not fully traced. General powers do not establish that direction alone solves every case. **Interest:** primary law, no empirical effectiveness claim.

## S09

Australian Government transport department. (2017; current landing page retrieved 2026). [*Whole journey guide*](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/infrastructure-transport-vehicles/transport-accessibility/whole-journey-guide).

**Read:** official scope, consultation and ministerial-endorsement description. **Design/fitness:** consensus guidance (VII), moderate for a whole-journey design framework, not a causal evaluation or service entitlement. The 2017 date concerns endorsement, not a new 2026 evaluation. Individual mobility barriers and governance boundaries matter; no optimum walking distance follows. **Interest:** public accessibility guidance developed with stakeholders. Guide annexes and local compliance were not audited.

## S10

UK Department for Transport. (2025, December 17). [*Demand responsive transport*](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/demand-responsive-transport/demand-responsive-transport).

**Read:** service-design, booking, procurement and success-measure sections. **Design/fitness:** official practitioner guidance (VII), moderate for design choices, no comparative causal effect. It describes both immediate and advance bookings and different pickup models. An accepted-booking arrival window is conceptually different from booking lead time. **Interest:** programme sponsor incorporating its commissioned evaluation, not independent replication of S11. **Transfer:** England's legislation does not apply in Victoria; costs, contracts and settlement patterns differ. Identity/date verified; legal details used only as overseas context.

## S11

University of the West of England, Bristol / UK Department for Transport. (2025, December 17). [*Rural Mobility Fund phase 1 evaluation: Final report*](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rural-mobility-fund-phase-1-evaluation-final-report), [PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69383a8a5cc812f50aa41e71/rmf-phase1-evaluation.pdf).

**Read:** executive summary; §§1–3, 7–9; relevant Appendix F design descriptions. **Design/fitness:** scheme monitoring and process interviews (VI), moderate for implementation problems, low for net causal impacts. Fifteen authorities supplied monitoring data through September 2024. **Author-acknowledged limits:** §2 describes missing demographic/comparator data; §7 warns that booking definitions differ across platforms, precluding direct scheme rankings. Phase 2 impact/value-for-money work is separate. Designs include virtual stops and targeted door-to-door access. **Interest:** university evaluation commissioned by programme funder, interviews include implementer self-report. Victoria transfer is conditional; no GBP costs converted into a Melton estimate.

## S12

Currie, G., & Fournier, N. (2020). [Why most DRT/Micro-Transits fail – What the survivors tell us about progress](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.retrec.2020.100895). *Research in Transportation Economics, 83*, 100895.

**Read:** publisher-indexed abstract/preview only; full text unavailable through inspected publisher access. **Design/fitness:** review of system survival/costs as described by authors; design/selection appraisal **not assessed (read scope: abstract/preview)**. Low for quantitative inference, useful adverse-evidence lead. No numerical failure rate carried into the verdicts. **Interest/funding:** not assessed without full text; no presumption of no conflicts. Crossref and Semantic Scholar title/year match; publisher preview confirms venue. Not an RCT or proof that Melton DRT must fail.

## S13

Alonso-González, M. J., Liu, T., Cats, O., Van Oort, N., & Hoogendoorn, S. (2018). [The potential of demand-responsive transport as a complement to public transport: An assessment framework and an empirical evaluation](https://doi.org/10.1177/0361198118790842). *Transportation Research Record, 2672*(8), 879–889.

**Read:** publisher HTML methodology, application, results, discussion, limitations, references and funding. **Design/fitness:** observational request-log case study with modelled alternative journeys (VI), moderate for a counterexample, low for general causal benefit. Nijmegen Breng flex is stop-to-stop. It uses 4,719 valid performed requests and 130 declined requests before further comparator exclusions; these are not a random resident sample. **Author-acknowledged limits:** application uses scheduled fixed-transit alternatives; conclusion lacks individual characteristics. **Reader-inferred:** user selection and simultaneous route removals prevent isolating a service-design effect for all residents. **Funding:** NWO; operator supplied data, acknowledged. Publisher states peer review and CC BY-NC 4.0. Institutional/PDF access failed, but publisher HTML was accessible. Crossref/S2 verify identity; online publication is August 2018, not the institutional page's placeholder January date.
