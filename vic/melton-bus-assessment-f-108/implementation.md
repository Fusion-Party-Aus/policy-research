## Comparable alternatives

No package is declared superior. Each row needs the same geography, destinations, time horizon and public resource envelope. Patronage alone is not access, welfare or car ownership change.

| Option | Potential advantage / reason to compare | Main risk and required comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Funded do-minimum | Avoid attributing already-announced Route 454/Melton South improvements to a new proposal (S17). | Verify actual implementation dates and present schedules; announcement ≠ delivery. |
| Targeted frequency and span | May fill off-peak/weekend and shift-work gaps with limited network disruption (S01, S03). | Price extra hours/fleet; measure access for trips currently impossible. |
| Frequent direct/grid routes plus local coverage | Can reduce indirect travel and build legible connections (S03–S04). | Longer walks/transfers and coverage loss; include subgroup losses, not only total boardings (S14). |
| Constrained timetable coordination | Uses existing service more effectively when shared routes are useful substitutes (new S05 analysis). | Rail/school/branch trade-offs, fleet/roster constraints, no assumption of zero cost. |
| DRT, community and assisted transport | Can fill thin-demand, first/last-leg or specific accessibility gaps (S10–S13). | Refusals, subsidy, aggregation, digital exclusion and capacity; DRT gains cannot be assumed for everyone. |
| Walking, crossings and interchange access | Can make existing service usable without changing frequency; whole-journey barriers are distinct from a stop radius (S09). | Capital/maintenance/road-space cost, disability usability and site safety require an audit. |
| Fare concessions integrated with services | Addresses cash affordability, retained as IV's alternative/complement (S03). | Fare revenue/administration/transfer rules and capacity; does not create a missing service. Independent fare-effect evaluation remains pending. |

## Cost and benefit workbook that is still needed

For each package specify route/zone geometry, operating span, headways, cycle/recovery/dead-running minutes, driver paid hours and reliefs, accessible fleet/depot needs, demand by time/user group, refusals and dispatch efficiency. Obtain lawful public contract/unit-cost information; no private trip or employment records are requested here.

**Analyst accounting identity:** annual incremental operating cost = added paid vehicle/driver hours × applicable unit costs + dispatch/planning/maintenance changes − genuinely released costs. Capital, renewal and transition costs remain separate. Report operator payments and public subsidy separately from fare revenue; fare savings are household incidence, not automatically a net social benefit. A contract renewal is a procurement opportunity, not a source of free resources.

Specify AUD price year, evaluation horizon, discount rate, demand growth, cost escalation, delivery risk and low/base/high scenarios before calculating a present value or benefit–cost ratio. These inputs are **not available**, so no NPV/BCR, fiscal saving or household car-disposal estimate is produced. Different walking speeds and transfer penalties should be sensitivity tests, not hidden universal weights. Any equity weighting must be declared and remain a human choice.

Benefits to measure include reachable jobs/essential services, reliable arrival within opening/work times, avoided waiting/booking, out-of-pocket affordability, access autonomy and distribution of losses. Do not add overlapping time/access benefits twice or treat gross car-running savings as fiscal revenue. Observed outcomes need before/after comparison with a credible comparator and dates for funded baseline changes.

Suggested administrative sequence: DTP validates baseline and contracts; council/road authorities review crossings/stops; operators supply feasible schedule/dispatch options using aggregate/public information; Treasury evaluates resource scenarios; affected users review accessibility and losses; government chooses/funds a pilot and independent evaluation plan. This is a recommendation for human consideration, not a commissioned project. New enforceable standards need a precise instrument, responsibility, exceptions, remedy, funding and review timetable. Unverified contract terms and appropriations remain legal/administrative gaps.
