## Main challenge findings

| Finding | Severity / effect | Revision or retained limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supporting an access objective can be mistaken for validating a particular service package | Major if left unqualified | Brief distinguishes measurement, local facts and intervention/cost claims; no full-policy endorsement. |

| Same-context appraisal can share author blind spots | Major limitation | No panel/independence claim; human review and targeted feasibility evidence remain required. |
| No whole-journey/cost model can rank alternatives | Major decision boundary | Comparable options and missing inputs supplied; no BCR/NPV or superior-package claim. |
| Quoted existing wording could imply new policy endorsement | Material communication risk | Prominent research labels; original snapshots/adopted wording preserved; new assessments clearly attributed. |

The strongest countercase to this assessment is that many local improvements may be inexpensive and useful even without an elaborate model. That is compatible with testing a constrained offset or service pilot; it does not supply evidence for universal zero-cost claims. Conversely, a financially subsidised DRT scheme may still have substantial social value. Failure to recover operating cost is not alone a welfare verdict. These qualifications are retained in the brief and implementation framework.

Review conclusion: the artifacts support **human review of bounded mixed verdicts and research priorities**. They do not establish a final service plan, statewide floor, legal entitlement or funding decision. Evidence gaps are an intended result where support cannot be established. Human reviewers should challenge claim selection, timetable substitution, incomplete literature coverage and the proposed next tranche before treating the research as a basis for policy amendment.
