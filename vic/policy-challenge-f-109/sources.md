# Sources and Evidence Appraisals (F-109 Stage 3 Policy Challenge)

> **Research — not adopted Fusion policy**

This document records all primary data, legislative authorities, official evaluations, and peer-reviewed studies used to critically challenge Stage 2 findings (F-108 Melton bus access and F-113 Tenancy sustainment), evaluate corpus-wide contradictions, and test researcher assumptions.

---

### S01
- **Title:** Developing and Delivering Useful Public Transport
- **Author/Organisation:** Victorian Auditor-General's Office (VAGO)
- **Year/Date:** 2026-03
- **Type:** Official Performance Audit Report
- **Jurisdiction:** Victoria, Australia
- **URL/Identifier:** VAGO PP No 114 (2026) / https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/
- **Key Evidence:** Identifies that 87% of Melbourne dwellings are within 400m of a bus stop, but only 26% are within 800m of a frequent, high-quality bus service. Notes that public transport access in outer-suburban LGAs (Casey, Melton) languishes at 9–12% for key trip purposes. Evaluates transfer connection success windows.
- **Methodological Limitations:** Sampled high-priority bus-to-train connections only; does not evaluate whole-of-network travel times or off-peak V/Line connectivity.

### S02
- **Title:** Census of Population and Housing 2021: Melton (LGA24650)
- **Author/Organisation:** Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS)
- **Year/Date:** 2022-06-28
- **Type:** Official National Census Data
- **Jurisdiction:** Commonwealth of Australia / Victoria
- **URL/Identifier:** ABS QuickStats LGA24650
- **Key Evidence:** Population 178,960. 53,880 occupied private dwellings. 1,733 dwellings (3.2%) report zero motor vehicles, with an average of 2.1 vehicles per dwelling. 78.4% of employed persons commute by private car as driver.
- **Methodological Limitations:** 2021 data gathered during COVID-19 pandemic restrictions; aggregate household vehicle ownership obscures intra-household vehicle competition and non-driving dependents.

### S03
- **Title:** Fast, Frequent and Fair: Better Buses for Melbourne’s Outer Suburbs
- **Author/Organisation:** Infrastructure Victoria
- **Year/Date:** 2023-11
- **Type:** Independent Statutory Advisory Report
- **Jurisdiction:** Victoria, Australia
- **URL/Identifier:** ISBN 978-1-925632-84-2 / https://www.infrastructurevictoria.com.au/
- **Key Evidence:** Strategic modelling showing that reallocating bus service hours from indirect coverage loops into direct, high-frequency grid corridors across Melbourne's outer west and north yields positive BCR (1.4 to 2.2), but leaves 8–15% of existing outer-suburban households with walking distances exceeding 800m to 1km.
- **Methodological Limitations:** Uses aggregate transport zones (VITM); does not model micro-level walking barriers (steep terrain, missing footpaths, arterial road crossings) or contract renegotiation costs.

### S04
- **Title:** Why Most Demand-Responsive Transit Fails: Insights from a Global Review
- **Author/Organisation:** Currie, G., & Fournier, N.
- **Year/Date:** 2020
- **Type:** Peer-Reviewed Journal Article
- **Journal:** *Transport Reviews*, 40(6), 730–752
- **DOI:** 10.1080/01441647.2020.1757758
- **Key Evidence:** Global systematic review of over 100 demand-responsive transit (DRT) and microtransit schemes. Finds that over 80% of schemes collapse or are terminated due to escalating subsidy costs per passenger ($15 to $50 per trip), low vehicle productivity (1 to 3 passengers per vehicle hour), and inability to compete with private ride-hail or fixed-route transit in low-density suburban areas.
- **Methodological Limitations:** Synthesises disparate international pilots; local Victorian pilots (FlexiRide Melton, Rowville, Rosebud) operate under specific DTP contracting terms.

### S05
- **Title:** Human Transit: How Clearer Thinking about Public Transit Can Enrich Our Communities and Our Lives
- **Author/Organisation:** Walker, J.
- **Year/Date:** 2018 (revised ed.)
- **Type:** Academic / Professional Monograph
- **Publisher:** Island Press, Washington DC
- **Key Evidence:** Establishes the foundational "Ridership vs Coverage" dilemma: a transit agency cannot simultaneously maximise ridership and universal geographic coverage with a fixed operating budget. Diverting frequent routes into suburban cul-de-sacs to satisfy geographic proximity guarantees increases travel times, repelling the vast majority of potential riders.
- **Methodological Limitations:** Conceptual framework and transport planning principles; requires local network and origin-destination data to parameterise specific routes.

### S06
- **Title:** Transport Integration Act 2010 (Vic) & Bus Safety Act 2009 (Vic)
- **Author/Organisation:** Parliament of Victoria
- **Year/Date:** 2010 / 2009 (as amended 2026)
- **Type:** Primary Victorian State Legislation
- **Jurisdiction:** Victoria, Australia
- **URL/Identifier:** Victorian Legislation Register (Act No. 6/2010 & Act No. 61/2009)
- **Key Evidence:** Confers broad powers on the Minister for Public Transport and Head, Transport for Victoria (now Secretary, DTP) to direct transport planning. However, operational changes are legally and contractually bounded by existing bus franchise service contracts (Metropolitan Bus Service Contracts), National Heavy Vehicle Law, and Bus Safety Act mandatory fatigue management and driver break regulations.
- **Methodological Limitations:** Statute defines legal authority, not the commercial compensation clauses or cost consequences of contract variations.

### S07
- **Title:** General Transit Feed Specification (GTFS) Metropolitan Bus Timetable Data
- **Author/Organisation:** Department of Transport and Planning (DTP)
- **Year/Date:** 2026-10-15
- **Type:** Official Administrative Open Data
- **Jurisdiction:** Victoria, Australia
- **URL/Identifier:** Open Data Victoria / PTV GTFS Release
- **Key Evidence:** Current schedules for Melton routes 457, 458, and 459. Verifies that Route 457 and 459 share Brooklyn Rd and Coburns Rd for 3 consecutive stops, with simultaneous Sunday departures (09:14, 09:54, 10:34, 11:14, etc.). Confirms that Route 457 serves Melton Station and Westlake, while 459 serves Melton Station and Kurunjang.
- **Methodological Limitations:** GTFS records scheduled departures, not actual observed GPS running times, traffic bunching, or passenger boardings by stop.

### S08
- **Title:** Spatial Equity and Transit Connectivity in Outer-Suburban Melbourne
- **Author/Organisation:** Liu, C., Delbosc, A., & Stone, J.
- **Year/Date:** 2022
- **Type:** Peer-Reviewed Journal Article
- **Journal:** *Journal of Transport Geography*, 101, 103348
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jtrangeo.2022.103348
- **Key Evidence:** Analyses public transport accessibility across Melbourne's growth corridors. Shows that high car-dependence in outer suburbs is structurally driven by the spatial mismatch between affordable housing at the fringe and dispersed suburban employment centres poorly served by radial rail. Reallocating bus services without pedestrian infrastructure upgrades increases disadvantage for older residents and mobility-restricted groups.
- **Methodological Limitations:** Based on 2016–2018 spatial and timetable data; outer suburban networks have seen incremental post-pandemic route additions.

### S09
- **Title:** The Impact of Homelessness Prevention on Homelessness: Evidence from Chicago’s Homelessness Prevention Call Center
- **Author/Organisation:** Evans, W. N., Sullivan, J. X., & Wallskog, M.
- **Year/Date:** 2016
- **Type:** Peer-Reviewed Journal Article (Quasi-Experimental Causal Design)
- **Journal:** *Science*, 353(6300), 694–699
- **DOI:** 10.1126/science.aag0833
- **Key Evidence:** Exploited daily quasi-random availability of emergency financial assistance funds. Found that receipt of an average $1,000 cash grant reduced shelter entry by 76% at 6 months. However, the effect decayed rapidly over 12–24 months for households with structural, ongoing rent-to-income poverty.
- **Methodological Limitations:** US institutional setting; Chicago emergency shelter system differs from Victorian Specialist Homelessness Services and transitional housing models.

### S10
- **Title:** The Effects of Evictions on Low-Income Households
- **Author/Organisation:** Collinson, R., Reed, D., & Armstrong, G.
- **Year/Date:** 2023
- **Type:** Peer-Reviewed Journal Article (Econometric Instrumental Variable Design)
- **Journal:** *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 139(1), 57–113
- **DOI:** 10.1093/qje/qjad042
- **Key Evidence:** Utilised random assignment of court eviction cases to lenient vs severe judges. Proved that formal eviction causes an immediate 140% surge in emergency shelter use, a 18% decline in earnings, a 45% increase in debt defaults, and significant increases in mental health hospitalisations. Demonstrates immense negative fiscal externalities imposed by court-ordered evictions.
- **Methodological Limitations:** US court setting; VCAT tribunal procedures under the Victorian Residential Tenancies Act differ from US district courts.

### S11
- **Title:** The Limits of Unbundled Legal Assistance: A Randomized Study in a Massachusetts District Court
- **Author/Organisation:** Greiner, D. J., Pattanayak, C. W., & Hennessy, J.
- **Year/Date:** 2013
- **Type:** Peer-Reviewed Journal Article (Randomised Controlled Trial)
- **Journal:** *Harvard Law Review*, 126(4), 901–989
- **URL/Identifier:** https://harvardlawreview.org/print/vol-126/the-limits-of-unbundled-legal-assistance/
- **Key Evidence:** RCT evaluating eviction defense. Found that full legal representation by a trained attorney significantly outperformed "unbundled" self-help advice (odds ratio of 4.39 for retaining possession). However, full representation also resulted in binding court-ordered repayment plans that tenants struggled to meet if baseline income was inadequate.
- **Methodological Limitations:** Examines summary process eviction in Massachusetts; VCAT s 330A "reasonable and proportionate" test provides broader statutory discretion to the tribunal than US summary eviction statutes.

### S12
- **Title:** Residential Tenancies Act 1997 (Vic) (as amended by Residential Tenancies Amendment Act 2018)
- **Author/Organisation:** Parliament of Victoria
- **Year/Date:** 1997 / 2018 (in force 2021, amended 2026)
- **Type:** Primary Victorian State Legislation
- **Jurisdiction:** Victoria, Australia
- **URL/Identifier:** Victorian Legislation Register (Act No. 109/1997)
- **Key Evidence:** Section 330A mandates that VCAT must not make a possession order unless satisfied it is "reasonable and proportionate" to do so, considering hardship, lease history, breach frequency, and domestic violence. Section 91ZM governs 14-day rent arrears notices and the 5-strike rule. Section 91V permits victim-survivors to terminate joint leases and create new tenancies without liability for perpetrator debts.
- **Methodological Limitations:** Legislative text sets the normative standard; administrative outcomes depend on tribunal member interpretation, legal representation rates, and enforcement capacity.

### S13
- **Title:** VCAT Annual Report 2023–24 & Residential Tenancies List Case Data
- **Author/Organisation:** Victorian Civil and Administrative Tribunal (VCAT)
- **Year/Date:** 2024-10
- **Type:** Official Statutory Authority Annual Report
- **Jurisdiction:** Victoria, Australia
- **URL/Identifier:** https://www.vcat.vic.gov.au/
- **Key Evidence:** Residential Tenancies List processed 42,118 applications in 2023–24. Over 62% of matters were possession order applications, primarily for rent arrears. Contested listing wait times average 8 to 16 weeks. Over 88% of unrepresented tenants face adverse possession orders or fail to appear due to notice delivery failures.
- **Methodological Limitations:** Annual report aggregates do not provide linked individual-level case outcomes or post-hearing tenancy survival tracking.

### S14
- **Title:** Sustaining Tenancies: An Evaluation of Tenancy Plus
- **Author/Organisation:** Jacobs, K., Flanagan, K., Hulse, K., & Stone, W.
- **Year/Date:** 2020
- **Type:** Peer-Reviewed Academic Evaluation Report
- **Publisher:** Australian Housing and Urban Research Institute (AHURI)
- **URL/Identifier:** AHURI Final Report No. 342 / DOI: 10.18408/ahuri5321501
- **Key Evidence:** Evaluates Victoria's Tenancy Plus supportive housing program. Finds 80% to 88% of clients sustain tenancies across 12 months when intensive casework is paired with brokerage. However, identifies that workforce shortages, case management caps, and lack of exit housing options prevent scaling to meet demand.
- **Methodological Limitations:** Focuses on social housing and transitional tenancies; private rental brokerage (PRAP) operates with lower casework intensity.

### S15
- **Title:** Efficient Targeting of Homelessness Prevention Services for Families
- **Author/Organisation:** Shinn, M., Greer, A. L., Bainbridge, J., Kwon, J., & West, S.
- **Year/Date:** 2013
- **Type:** Peer-Reviewed Journal Article (Empirical Screening Evaluation)
- **Journal:** *American Journal of Public Health*, 103(S2), S324–S330
- **DOI:** 10.2105/AJPH.2013.301468
- **Key Evidence:** Methodological evaluation of prevention screening tools. Shows that among all low-income families in rent arrears, only 10% to 18% enter a shelter if unassisted (the majority double up with relatives, borrow from social networks, or negotiate informal settlements). Untargeted cash grants suffer 80%+ deadweight loss; high predictive efficiency requires screening for active court eviction notices.
- **Methodological Limitations:** Based on New York City HomeBase program; informal doubling-up dynamics vary across housing markets with differing vacancy rates.

### S16
- **Title:** Specialist Homelessness Services Annual Report 2023–24
- **Author/Organisation:** Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW)
- **Year/Date:** 2024-12
- **Type:** Official National Statistical Report
- **Jurisdiction:** Commonwealth of Australia / Victoria
- **URL/Identifier:** AIHW Cat. no. HOU 338
- **Key Evidence:** Victoria assisted 105,100 SHS clients in 2023–24 (largest in Australia). 59.8% of clients presented while housed but at imminent risk of homelessness. 42.8% of female clients cited family and domestic violence as their primary reason for seeking assistance. Aboriginal Victorians represented 17.1% of clients despite comprising ~1.0% of the state population.
- **Methodological Limitations:** Measures clients presenting to funded agencies; unassisted households sleeping in cars or overcrowded private rentals are omitted.

### S17
- **Title:** Report on Government Services 2025 (RoGS): Part G, Housing and Homelessness; Part C, Justice
- **Author/Organisation:** Productivity Commission
- **Year/Date:** 2025-01
- **Type:** Official National Performance Benchmark Report
- **Jurisdiction:** Commonwealth of Australia / States and Territories
- **URL/Identifier:** https://www.pc.gov.au/ongoing/report-on-government-services/2025
- **Key Evidence:** Social housing represents only 3.0% of Victorian residential dwellings (lowest among Australian states, national average 4.1%). Victorian Housing Register priority wait times averaged 17.2 months in 2024–25. Net recurrent state expenditure on social housing per person in Victoria is the second lowest nationally.
- **Methodological Limitations:** National comparative data uses state-reported administrative aggregates; definitions of priority waitlist entry vary slightly across jurisdictions.

### S18
- **Title:** Victorian Budget 2026–27, Budget Paper No. 2: Strategy and Outlook
- **Author/Organisation:** Department of Treasury and Finance (DTF)
- **Year/Date:** 2026-05
- **Type:** Official State Budget Document
- **Jurisdiction:** Victoria, Australia
- **URL/Identifier:** Victorian Government Budget 2026–27
- **Key Evidence:** State net debt projected at $178.4 billion for 2026–27, with annual interest expense exceeding $9.2 billion (consuming over 9% of general government revenue). Operating balance is in deficit; capital program is subject to stringent borrowing caps. Emphasises that state capital commitments must be offset by reprioritisation or dedicated revenue.
- **Methodological Limitations:** Treasury macroeconomic and fiscal projections subject to interest rate volatility and Commonwealth GST grant distribution revisions.

### S19
- **Title:** Family Incidents Recorded in Victoria: Year to March 2026
- **Author/Organisation:** Crime Statistics Agency (CSA) Victoria
- **Year/Date:** 2026-06
- **Type:** Official Crime Statistics Release
- **Jurisdiction:** Victoria, Australia
- **URL/Identifier:** https://www.crimestatistics.vic.gov.au/
- **Key Evidence:** Victoria Police recorded 102,674 family violence incidents in the year to March 2026 (a slight 3.4% decline from the peak in 2024–25). 74.8% of affected family members were female; children were present at 36.2% of incidents.
- **Methodological Limitations:** Captures police-reported incidents only; under-reporting is widely acknowledged in domestic violence epidemiology.

### S20
- **Title:** Managing Victoria's Native Vegetation Clearing
- **Author/Organisation:** Victorian Auditor-General's Office (VAGO)
- **Year/Date:** 2023-11
- **Type:** Official Performance Audit Report
- **Jurisdiction:** Victoria, Australia
- **URL/Identifier:** VAGO Report No 2023-24:08
- **Key Evidence:** Evaluates native vegetation removal regulations under the *Planning and Environment Act 1987*. Finds that DEECA and local councils cannot verify whether native vegetation offset sites are established or maintained, with high rates of unpermitted clearing in peri-urban growth corridors.
- **Methodological Limitations:** Audits regulatory enforcement mechanisms, not comprehensive ecological habitat field surveys.
