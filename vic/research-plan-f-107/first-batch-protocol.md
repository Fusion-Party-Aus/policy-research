# B1 proposal: useful bus access in Melton

> **Research — not adopted Fusion policy**

**Status:** proposed protocol v0.1, 17 October 2026 AEDT; not a completed review. **Author:** Policy Researcher, AI-assisted. **Owner of acceptance/promotion:** humans and Mika under F-105. No searches below have been executed as a stage-2 review; stage-1 discovery is separately logged.

## Question and decision boundary

Policy-led context question: for Melton residents who have limited access to a car or usable public transport, which practical package most improves access to jobs and essential services, compared with the current service, within a transparent operating/capital resource envelope? Compare fixed-route frequency/span changes, timetable coordination, demand-responsive/community transport, interchange/walking/accessibility improvements, fare concessions and a do-minimum comparator. Do not assume that a grid, a distance standard or door-to-door service is best.

Policy-led question: do the facts and mechanisms in **Fix Transit Deserts**, **Set a Transit Access Standard**, **Stagger the Timetables**, and **End FlexiRide's Fake Flexibility** survive that comparison? This is a Melton case study using metropolitan comparators; it cannot establish a single statewide service standard. Rural settings are a transferability boundary, not excluded from the eventual programme.

Population/context: Melton municipality, with distinct attention to people with disability, older residents, young people, low-income/carless households and shift workers; aggregate public data only. Outcomes: destinations reachable within stated travel-time budgets, whole-journey time/reliability, walking barriers, service span, booking lead time/refusal, out-of-pocket cost, operating/capital costs and distribution of gains/losses. Patronage is intermediate evidence; it is not a substitute for access or welfare. Use one named weekday and one weekend service date from a documented current timetable release; record disruptions and separate planned schedules from observed operation.

## Retrieval, screening, appraisal and stopping rules

Use the separate proposed [screening rules](screening-protocol.md) and [configuration](screening-config.json); no study screening starts until human confirmation. This deliverable is a bounded research-pass design, not a registered systematic review or PRISMA result. Preserve full search exports and identifiers; deduplicate DOI only when title/year are compatible, otherwise exact/normalised title+year+author for candidate duplicates. Retain duplicate-to-master relationships and all source ranks. Search limits are coverage truncation, not exclusions.

1. Register up to 12 atomic claims and a public-data context baseline. Search all six lanes/source groups above. Inspect at most the first 50 returned records per query, deduplicate and log the remainder as uninspected. Query budget is 18 query runs; the first table row contains six runs, second two, third two, fourth four, fifth two, leaving two runs for replication/targeted gaps. Record unavailable native catalogues as inaccessible, never as zero hits.
2. Retrieve up to 40 external documents for deep extraction/appraisal, with at least two relevant adverse/null/alternative sources sought for each mechanism family. Do not manufacture a quota of findings: report when none is found. More candidate records can remain pending without affecting included-study counts. This cap is not a claim of saturation.
3. One backward-reference and one forward-citation pass for the five most decision-relevant sources, counting against the same document/time caps. Prioritise contrasting designs/outcomes, not agreement. Keep primary empirical datasets separate from multiple reports using them.
4. Stop after 12 research hours, 40 appraised documents or completion of the declared lanes plus the citation pass, whichever comes first. Log which queries, candidates, claims and outcomes remain unassessed. A contradiction with material decision impact triggers a targeted query within the budget; if unresolved at the cap, conclude insufficient evidence and propose a separate bounded follow-up.
5. If timetables, dispatch data, cost bases or source texts are inaccessible, use the accessible portion, document exceptions and do not infer observed reliability from scheduled service. No credentials, paid access, private passenger data, legal action, policy amendment or live service changes are authorised.

Assess design and claim fitness separately: contemporaneous law for legal assertions; defined official data for baselines; plausible counterfactual/evaluation for effects; transparent assumptions and sensitivity for economics. Extract year, population, jurisdiction, comparator, time horizon, sample, effect/uncertainty, costs/price year, funding/COI, bias, disability/distributional impacts, and Victoria transferability. No effect size from an abstract without checking the relevant methods/result context; abstract-only evidence is visibly limited.

Return the baseline, claim/source matrix, comparable options table, coverage/decision ledger, source-access exceptions, negative/contradictory findings and ranked follow-up questions. Verdicts belong to specific claims; a bundle can have mixed outcomes. Human review decides whether to accept the tranche, extend research or change any adopted policy.
