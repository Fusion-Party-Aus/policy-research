## Ranking rule and bounded batches

Scores are provisional **research triage judgments** on a 1–3 scale, not measurements of actual harm or validated evidence quality. **S (significance)**: 3 = potentially large fiscal, access or rights stakes; 2 = narrower institutional scope; 1 = limited consequential scope. **W (evidence weakness signal)**: 3 = exposed unsupported/universal mechanism, unresolved costing or no linked research; 2 = linked analysis exists but independent verification is pending; 1 = independently assessed evidence with minor residual issues. No W=1 is assigned here. Missing linkage alone is not proof of weak evidence. **F (feasibility)**: 3 = accessible official baselines and a tight comparison; 2 = multi-agency, contracts or modelling required; 1 = core material unavailable. Sum = S+W+F; ties favour shared reusable sources and readily falsifiable claims. Costs and rights can justify moving a lower score earlier; record that human choice.

Record unsearched, retrieved, screened, full-text obtained, extracted, appraised and assessed as separate states. A source accessed once is not an assessed policy. Count unique external studies independently from repeated citation instances and policy coverage. Publish no “percentage complete” for unregistered claims.

Every tranche returns an evidence matrix, counterevidence/alternatives table, reproducible search/decision log, access exceptions and a prioritised residual gap list. Allowed outcomes include well supported, right objective but weak mechanism, unsubstantiated claim, overlooked consequences, better alternative and insufficient evidence. Conclusions must name the specific claim and evidence boundary. Human value trade-offs and formal policy adoption remain separate.
